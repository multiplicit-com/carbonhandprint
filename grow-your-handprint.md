The best things you can do cut your own emissions and also make it easier for someone else to cut theirs.

## Start with your biggest impact

Perhaps it is your commute, your heating, or the packaging your weekly shop comes in. You don't need to give up travelling or eating to make simple changes that make a big difference. Then think about who else you could bring with you.

| Footprint step (just you) | Handprint step (you plus others) |
|---|---|
| Cycle to work | Set up a bike buddy scheme or ask for secure bike parking at work |
| Switch to a green energy tariff | Help a neighbour, parent or your workplace switch too |
| Eat more seasonal, local food | Start a veg box share, or cook for friends and share the recipes |
| Repair instead of replace | Volunteer at, or start, a local repair café |
| Cut your own waste | Ask your school, club or office to set up proper recycling and refill points |
| Reduce flights | Make the case for video meetings or rail travel at work |

## Reduce consumption

Reducing consumption is the easiest way to help the environment. Less waste means less landfill and less energy spent transporting it. Re-use packaging, think about food miles, and buy things built to last.

## Use your voice

Governments at every level make the decisions that affect us all. Every environmental cause needs the support of politicians and representatives to move forward.

- Write to or meet your local councillor or MP about a specific local issue.
- Vote, and ask candidates where they stand on climate.
- Support campaigns for cleaner transport, energy and planning where you live.

## Use your work

For a lot of people, work is where they can make the biggest difference. If there's a lower-carbon way of doing something, suggest it.

## Share it

Your carbon handprint can be measured by positive experiences just as much as by the greenhouse gases saved. Share what you're doing with friends. Helping the environment should be fun and inclusive.
