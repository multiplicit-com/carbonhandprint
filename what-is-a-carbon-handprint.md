## The short version

A **carbon footprint** counts the greenhouse gases caused by your own activities: the fuel you burn, the electricity you use, the things you buy.

A **carbon handprint** looks outward. It is the positive climate impact you create beyond yourself: the emissions you help other people avoid, the habits you pass on, the projects you start, the decisions you influence.

## Why a second measure?

For years the climate conversation has focused on footprints. That matters, but it has two weaknesses.

- **It can feel hopeless.** A footprint can only ever get smaller, and it never reaches zero just by living. For a lot of people that message turns into guilt, then inaction.
- **It stops at your front door.** Some of the most useful things anyone does for the climate happen outside their own consumption: persuading an employer to switch energy supplier, starting a repair café, designing a more efficient product, voting, campaigning.

The handprint gives those things a name, and gives people a reason to keep going. It was never meant to replace the footprint or let anyone off the hook. It's just a different way in.

## Two ways the idea is used

### 1. Personal and community handprints

This is the broad, everyday meaning, and the one this site started with. Your handprint is your contribution to the environment. It includes:

- **Changes you make that spread**, like cycling to work and getting two colleagues to try it.
- **Things you start**, like a community energy scheme, a tool library or a school garden.
- **Influence you use**, at work, in your community, or with local and national politicians.
- **Positive experiences**: the exercise from a cycle commute or the new meals you discover by eating seasonally. A handprint can be measured in experiences as well as in tonnes of CO₂.

### 2. Product and organisation handprints

Since 2018, researchers at [VTT Technical Research Centre of Finland](https://www.vttresearch.com/) and [LUT University](https://www.lut.fi/) have published a formal method for companies, collected at [handprint.fi](https://handprint.fi/). Our [frameworks and methods](/methods/) page compares it with the other main approaches. Here a carbon handprint is the reduction in a *customer's* footprint that happens because they use your product or service instead of a baseline alternative.

A simple example: a washing-machine maker that runs its factory on renewables shrinks its own footprint. If it also makes a machine that washes well at 20 °C, it grows its handprint, because every household that uses it emits less.

In 2021 the method was widened into an **environmental handprint**, which covers air quality, water, nutrients and resource use as well as carbon. Companies such as Neste and [Nokia](/articles/2026/11/nokia-and-the-handprint-of-connectivity/), both partners in developing the original method, now talk about their handprints alongside their footprints.

<div class="note">
<strong>A word of caution.</strong> A handprint should never be used to cancel out a footprint on paper. Claims about avoided emissions are easy to exaggerate. A good handprint claim says what it is compared against, is honest about uncertainty, and sits next to an honest footprint, never in place of one.
</div>

## Footprint and handprint side by side

| | Carbon footprint | Carbon handprint |
|---|---|---|
| What it counts | Emissions you cause | Emissions you help avoid, and positive change you enable |
| Direction | Make it smaller | Make it bigger |
| Scope | Your own activities | Your influence on others |
