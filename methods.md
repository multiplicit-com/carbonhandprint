Most methods share the same core idea. You compare the footprint of a baseline, meaning what people would have used anyway, with the footprint of your product or action. The difference is the handprint. Where the methods differ is in what they count, and in how strict they are about the baseline.

## VTT and LUT University: Carbon Handprint Guide

The most widely used handprint method comes from [VTT Technical Research Centre of Finland](https://www.vttresearch.com/) and [LUT University](https://www.lut.fi/). It defines a carbon handprint as the cut in a customer's footprint that happens because they use your product instead of a baseline. It sets out the steps for choosing that baseline, calculating both footprints with life cycle assessment, and communicating the result.

- [Carbon Handprint Guide](https://sarjaweb.vtt.fi/julkaisut/muut/2018/Carbon_Handprint_Guide.pdf) (2018), the first edition, developed with companies including Nokia, Neste and KONE.
- [Carbon handprint guide, V. 2.0](https://publications.vtt.fi/julkaisut/muut/2021/Carbon_handprint_guide_2021.pdf) (2021), the current version, which also works for the wider environmental handprint: water, air, nutrients and resources.
- [Carbon handprint: an approach to assess the positive climate impacts of products](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652618329664) (2019), the peer-reviewed paper behind the method, in the *Journal of Cleaner Production*.
- [handprint.fi](https://handprint.fi/), the home of the VTT and LUT handprint projects.

## WBCSD: Guidance on Avoided Emissions

Large companies often call their handprint "avoided emissions". The [World Business Council for Sustainable Development](https://www.wbcsd.org/Imperatives/Climate-Action/Resources/Guidance-on-Avoided-Emissions) publishes the main guidance on measuring them. It defines avoided emissions as the positive impact found by comparing the emissions of a low-carbon solution with a scenario where that solution isn't used. Version 2.0 (July 2025) adds eligibility tests, guidance on choosing the reference scenario and reporting templates, aligned with the GHG Protocol, IPCC, PCAF and ISO. It aims to keep claims credible, transparent and conservative.

## ITU-T L.1480: digital technology and other sectors

The UN's telecoms agency, the ITU, publishes [Recommendation L.1480](https://www.itu.int/rec/T-REC-L.1480/en), "Enabling the Net Zero transition". It covers how to assess the effect that information and communication technology has on the emissions of other sectors, such as remote working, smart grids or connected logistics. The current edition is from July 2025.

## Nokia

Nokia was a partner in the original VTT and LUT work and now reports its handprint alongside its footprint. Its [climate transition plan](https://www.nokia.com/sites/default/files/2025-06/climate-transition-plan.pdf) (2025) sets out the products it says cut energy use and emissions for its customers. There's more in [Nokia and the handprint of connectivity](/blog/2026/11/nokia-and-the-handprint-of-connectivity/).

## Gregory Norris: handprinting and NetPositive

Gregory Norris, first at the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health and later at MIT, developed handprinting as a way to count the footprint reductions a person or organisation brings about in others. The goal is to become "net positive", with a handprint bigger than your footprint, measured against business as usual.

- [Handprint-Based NetPositive Assessment](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.mit.edu/dist/0/1158/files/2025/01/Norris-2015-Handprint-Based-NetPositive-Assessment_2.pdf) (2015).
- [SHINE handprint methodological framework](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11367-021-01874-5) (2021), *International Journal of Life Cycle Assessment*.

## ISO 14067: the footprint underneath

A handprint is worked out from two footprints, so it is only as good as those footprints. [ISO 14067:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/71206.html), *Carbon footprint of products: requirements and guidelines for quantification*, is the international standard for calculating a product's footprint. It builds on the life cycle assessment standards ISO 14040 and 14044, which the handprint methods above also rely on. ISO 14067 doesn't cover handprints or offsetting itself, and a revision is under way.

## The International Carbon Handprint Award

The [International Carbon Handprint Award](https://carbonhandprintaward.com/carbon-handprint-method/), run by Finland's Climate Leadership Coalition and the Vancouver Economic Commission, uses the VTT and LUT method for entries. It [first ran in 2022](https://news.cision.com/miltton/r/schneider-electric-and-ssab-receive-world-s-first-international-carbon-handprint-awards-in-new-york,c3633932), when Schneider Electric and SSAB won.

## What good methods have in common

- **State the baseline.** A handprint means nothing unless you say what it is compared against.
- **Report it separately.** A handprint sits next to your footprint. It never cancels it out.
- **Be conservative.** Claims about avoided emissions are easy to inflate, so good methods ask for transparency about assumptions and uncertainty.
